# Copperbell Hollow — Story Bible
### *The Buried Age*
Minecraft Forge 1.20.1 · private modpack · cozy-first, tech-deep

---

## 1. Pack name and tagline

**Candidates**

1. **Copperbell Hollow** — warm, place-first, sounds like a town you'd want to live in. The bell is both a cozy object (the town bell) and a machine part (the resonator at the heart of the buried works).
2. **The Kindling Company** — leans industrial-mystery; strong for the tech lane, cold for the cozy lane.
3. **Hearth & Hollow** — very cozy, but says nothing about the machine age underneath.

**Picked: Copperbell Hollow** — subtitle *The Buried Age*. It reads cozy on the title screen, and by Act V the player learns "Copperbell" was never a poetic name; it was the model number stamped on the great resonator sleeping under the town square.

**Tagline:** *A cozy little town, sitting on top of a sleeping machine.*

---

## 2. Premise (shown on first join)

> Your great-aunt Nan Copperbell left you a valley.
>
> Not a fortune — a valley. A crooked inn with the lights off, a mill with a broken wheel, an orchard gone wild, and eleven people who still live here because they don't know where else to go. The letter in your hand says the town used to be famous for something, and that she was sorry she never finished fixing it.
>
> There's a second letter under the first, in different handwriting, and it isn't addressed to you at all. It says: **"The Works are still down there. They're only sleeping."**
>
> Put your bell-post in the ground wherever you want home to be. Light one lantern. We'll go one step at a time from here — the book will always tell you the next thing to do.

The quest book opens on **one chapter, one pinned objective**. There is never a second thing to pick.

## 3. The setting

**The valley.** Copperbell Hollow is a green bowl of a valley with a stream down the middle, an orchard on the south slope, a marsh at the east end where the Ribbits live, and a scree-covered collapsed pithead on the north wall that everyone in town politely does not look at. There's a crossroads with a signpost, a stone tower with no bell in it, and an inn called the Half-Lit Lantern that has been half-lit for nine years.

**What is broken.**
- The **bell tower** is empty. The bell was taken down "for repairs" and never came back.
- The **mill wheel** is snapped; the miller left; nobody in town can make flour, so nobody bakes.
- The **bridge** to the north wall is out, which is convenient, because that's where the pithead is.
- The **greenhouse** behind the orchard has no glass and no heat, so the valley only eats what the current season allows.
- The **Records House** roof fell in and Ottoline is slowly drying out books one at a time.
- Below all of it: the **Undertown** — flooded galleries, cold boilers, dead conveyor lines, and the Emberwright Works. The generation before Nan built a machine age here, and then one bad day at the pithead ended it, and the town decided to be a farming town instead and never talk about it.

**What restoring it means.** The cozy lane puts the town back on its feet — food, light, homes, neighbors, festivals. The tech lane goes down and wakes the Works back up. The story's argument is that the town was never *on top of* the machine — the machine was built *to serve* the town, and it stopped because nobody was left who wanted anything from it. By Act V the two lanes are the same project: the Works exist to heat the greenhouse, light the lanes, pull the ore, and ring the bell.

**Keeping a non-creative player oriented.**
- **A world border, opened one act at a time.** Act I: 512-block radius centred on the Town Waystone — small enough to feel like a yard. Act II: 1,500. Act III: 4,000. Act IV: 12,000. Act V: removed. Each expansion is a finale reward with a title card ("The road east is clear"), so the world never feels like an unbounded blank.
- **Waystones are the map.** Every act hands out named waystones (Home, The Lantern, The Mill, Marsh Landing, The Pithead, Undertown, The Works, Winterhold Orchard). Travel is a menu, not a navigation problem.
- **Xaero's waypoints are set for her.** Every quest that says "go somewhere" gives the waypoint as part of the reward, so "where is that" is never a question.
- **Explorer's Compass and Nature's Compass are given early and are always the answer.** Any quest that says "find X" also says "use the Explorer's Compass and pick X from the list." She never has to wander hoping.
- **Torchmaster torch given in Act I** so the homestead is permanently safe. Night is atmosphere, not a threat, at home.
- **One chapter open at a time.** Completed chapters collapse into a "Town Story" archive. The book never shows a wall.

## 4. Cast

Eight NPCs, all placed with Easy NPC (name, skin, dialog). All are warm. Nobody is a villain; the closest thing to an antagonist is a locked door and one old man's guilt.

### Marigold "Goldie" Wren — innkeeper of the Half-Lit Lantern — **cozy**
Round, loud, wears three cardigans. Runs an inn with one guest (herself).
**Want:** to hear the common room noisy again.
**Arc:** (1) Pours you a free bowl of soup the night you arrive and refuses payment, because "you're the only new face in nine years." (2) Reopens the kitchen once you get her flour and a proper stove — starts feeding the whole town daily, and starts charging again, which she is thrilled about. (3) Hosts the Lantern Festival in Act V and puts your name on the guest board above hers.

### Bram Tallow — blacksmith and tinker — **both**
Big hands, quiet, keeps a broken brass gear on his workbench he won't explain.
**Want:** to prove his hands are still worth something in a world that used to have machines.
**Arc:** (1) Repairs your tools for free and tells you the pithead is "not for visiting." (2) Builds his first Create gearbox with you at the mill and laughs out loud — the gear on his bench was his father's, from the Works. (3) Takes Little Tam as an apprentice and moves his forge down into the Undertown workshop, the first person to live below in forty years.

### Ottoline "Otto" Vex — archivist of the Records House — **tech**
Ink-stained, talks fast, has a theory about everything and is right about most of them.
**Want:** to read the Emberwright logs and know what actually happened.
**Arc:** (1) Gives you Nan's soaked journal and asks for nothing in exchange, which is suspicious. (2) Decodes the pressure plates in the Undertown and realizes the Works were shut down deliberately, not destroyed. (3) Publishes the true founding record and admits Nan asked her to keep it quiet — then names *you* the current keeper of it.

### Pip Ribbit — courier from the marsh village — **cozy**
A Ribbit in a too-large satchel. Extremely earnest. Hums while walking.
**Want:** a mail route again, because a village with no post is just houses.
**Arc:** (1) Turns up at your door with nine years of undelivered letters, all soggy. (2) You rebuild the post box and he runs his first real route, which takes him three days and he is proud beyond measure. (3) The marsh village joins the Hollow's festival for the first time, and Pip carries the invitation himself.

### Hollis Ferrow — retired quarry foreman — **tech**
Lives alone in a hut by the collapsed pithead. Sharp, tired, not unkind.
**Want:** to be forgiven for the day the shaft flooded.
**Arc:** (1) Won't discuss the pithead, will discuss the weather at length. (2) Hands over his pump schematics when you show him the mill is turning again — proof somebody can be trusted with moving parts. (3) Walks back down into the Deep with you in Act IV, for the first time in forty years, and turns the master valve himself.

### Clementine Ash — orchardist and farmer — **cozy**
Mud on the knees, opinions about apples, keeps a goat named Governor.
**Want:** an orchard that survives all four seasons instead of two.
**Arc:** (1) Gives you seeds and a starter plot because "growing things is the only thing here that still works." (2) You reglaze and heat the greenhouse; she grows summer crops in the snow and cries a little. (3) She hosts the Harvest Table in Act V — one long table, every dish in the pack, everybody sits.

### Doctor Quill Sable — apothecary, beekeeper, herbalist — **both**
Soft-spoken, precise, keeps a beehive on the surgery roof that has been empty for years.
**Want:** to find out what made the valley's bees leave.
**Arc:** (1) Trades you remedies for foraged herbs, mentions offhand that the bees left the same year the Works closed. (2) Discovers the Emberwright vents were still slowly venting under the orchard, souring the blossom — and that fixing the Works properly is what fixes the bees. (3) The Bloom: the orchard flowers, the hives fill, and she names the first new queen after Nan.

### Little Tam — a kid with a duck — **cozy**
Eight years old. Duck is named Duchess. Follows you everywhere once you're interesting.
**Want:** a treehouse, a pet, and a job.
**Arc:** (1) Asks you to help find Duchess (Duchess is behind the inn). (2) You build the treehouse; Tam declares it the "Second Records House" and stores rocks in it. (3) Becomes Bram's apprentice and the town's official Bell Ringer, and rings the Copperbell in the finale.

## 5. The two lanes and how they gate each other

**Cozy lane (the wife's spine):** cook → feed → befriend → decorate → explore → celebrate. Farmer's Delight and Cooking for Blockheads kitchens, Aquaculture fishing, Serene Seasons crops, animals and pets, Handcrafted / Macaw's / Supplementaries decorating, Let's Do Bakery / Vinery / HerbalBrews / Candlelight shops, structures found with Explorer's Compass, Bountiful bounty board as the endless optional loop.

**Tech lane (Josh's spine):** Create → Thermal → Applied Energistics 2 → Bigger Reactors + QuarryPlus, in that order, each stage excavated out of the Buried Age rather than researched from nothing.

They are **braided, not parallel**. Neither lane can finish an act alone. Concrete dependencies:

1. **Hot food unlocks the dig.** (Act III) Hollis will not let anyone into the flooded shaft without three days of provisions for the crew. The tech lane's shaft-drain quest is hard-locked behind the cozy lane delivering a stocked Cooking for Blockheads pantry with nine cooked Farmer's Delight meals. Machines wait on lunch.
2. **The Create windmill lights the greenhouse.** (Act III) Serene Seasons winter kills the cozy crop plots. The greenhouse only produces out-of-season if it has heat and light — supplied by a Create water wheel / windmill driving a heater array. The cozy player's whole Act IV farm depends on the tech player finishing rotational power.
3. **Foraging finds the blueprints.** (Act II and IV) Every Create and Thermal schematic is a physical **Emberwright Plate** found in Lootr chests inside ruined structures — When Dungeons Arise, Towns and Towers, Dungeons and Taverns, Repurposed Structures, YUNG's. The cozy player locates them with the Explorer's Compass because that lane's quests send her out to see the world. The tech player literally cannot proceed past andesite-tier Create without plates the cozy lane brings home.
4. **Thermal steam runs the bakery and bathhouse.** (Act IV) Goldie's bread ovens, the Vinery press, and the town bathhouse are all defined as "hot lines" fed from a Thermal Dynamics fluid network. Cozy shop upgrades are gated on the tech player having an energy cell with charge in it.
5. **The AE2 terminal is the town's order book.** (Act IV) Once the ME network is online, the Bountiful bounty board and the townsfolk's daily requests auto-fill from network stock. Every cozy delivery quest after that point becomes one click instead of a scavenger hunt — the tech lane's reward *is* the cozy lane's convenience.
6. **The quarry supplies the town square.** (Act V) Rebuilding the square, the bell tower, and the festival grounds needs thousands of blocks. QuarryPlus + Geolosys deposits make that a background hum instead of a mining trip. The cozy finale is physically impossible without the tech finale.
7. **The reactor's waste heat is the hot springs and the winter orchard.** (Act V) The Bigger Reactors build is framed as "the town's furnace," not "a power plant." Its coolant loop terminates in Quill's bathhouse and Clementine's winter orchard.
8. **Bees gate the reactor's fuel line.** (Act IV→V) Quill's honey-wax seals are the only thing that stop the Emberwright vents leaking into the orchard. The tech player cannot get the "vents sealed" stage — required before the reactor is allowed to run — until the cozy player has restored the hives.

## 7. Reward philosophy applied

The rule: **every reward shortens the next task.** No reward is a trophy only. Three worked examples:

**1. Act I Q15 → Act I Q16.** Bringing Bram 8 iron rewards **Vein Mining plus two Storage Drawers and a barrel**. The very next quest sends you into the inn cellar and then straight into Act II's andesite grind. Vein Mining turns "mine 32 stone and 16 iron" from a twenty-minute chore into a two-minute one, and the drawers mean you never walk home to dump inventory. Josh's stated hatred of the early grind is answered inside the first hour: the game hands you the shortcut *before* the task that needs it, not after.

**2. Act III Q16 → Act IV Q1–Q3.** Laying Steam 'n' Rails track from the Undertown up to the pithead rewards a **working ore train**. Act IV opens with Thermal machines that eat ore in bulk and a Geolosys deposit that produces it in bulk. Without the train that's a hauling nightmare; with it, the "go get 64 ore chunks" quest is a matter of walking to the platform. The dependency is deliberate — the reward for finishing the fun engineering puzzle is the deletion of the boring logistics that would otherwise follow it.

**3. Act IV Q17 → every cozy delivery quest for the rest of the game.** Linking the AE2 terminal to the town's order board rewards **auto-filled bounties**. Before this, "bring Goldie 12 pumpkin pies" means baking 12 pumpkin pies. After it, the ME network already has them and the quest is a single click at the board. The cozy player gets the direct, visible payoff of the tech player's biggest build — and the tech player gets to watch his network become the thing that makes his wife's quests instant. That is the whole braid in one quest.

*(Corollaries used throughout: Sophisticated Backpack upgrades always land right before a long trip; every waystone is given before the walk that needs it, never after; Torchmaster arrives in Act I so night is never a setback; Lootr means a shared structure is never "already looted" for the person who arrives second; Corpse means a death is never a lost run.)*

## 8. Multiplayer: second teams and late joiners

**FTB Teams shape.** Josh and his wife share one team, **Copperbell Hollow**, so quest progress and rewards are pooled — she never blocks on his tech chapter and he never blocks on her cooking chapter; the shared act completion is what advances. Friends may either join that team or form their own.

**A second team is a second homestead, not a second story.** A new team runs the same five acts, but the fiction frames them as **another family answering another of Nan's letters** — she wrote several, on the theory that one of them would stick. Their Act I places *their* Homestead Waystone, and the same eight NPCs are shared world NPCs who greet whoever arrives. The town only needs restoring once, so:
- **Town-state quests** (mill wheel, greenhouse glass, bell tower) are marked one-per-world. If another team already did it, the quest auto-completes for the new team with a short line — *"The mill was already turning when you got here. Bram says you should meet whoever did it."* — and still pays the reward, because the reward is the shortcut, not the trophy.
- **Personal quests** (your shelter, your field, your kitchen, your fishing, your treehouse for Tam) always run fresh. Every team gets its own cozy build-out.
- **Lootr** means every player opens their own copy of every structure chest, so no one is ever punished for arriving late to a dungeon.

**A late joiner mid-Act-IV.** They are given a **catch-up chapter, not a backlog**: three short quests (place your waystone, cook one meal, meet three neighbors) that grant the KubeJS stages the world has already unlocked, a starter kit matched to the current act, and every waystone the world has activated. The book then drops them straight into the pinned objective the rest of the team is on. Nobody replays four acts to be useful. In fiction, Pip delivers their letter late — *"It got wet. It's been getting wet for nine years. I am so sorry."*

**Roles are suggested, never enforced.** The quest book tags each quest cozy/tech/both, and the Act chapter shows two pinned objectives when a team has more than one member — one cozy, one tech — so two people can always be busy at once on the same act. A solo player sees only one pin at a time and the act simply takes longer.

**Simple Voice Chat** is the town's ambience: proximity chat means the Harvest Table finale is people actually sitting around a table talking, which is the emotional payoff the whole pack is built toward.

## 9. Journal — sample entries (Patchouli, *Nan's Soaked Journal*)

**Entry 1 — "The Half-Lit Lantern" (given Act I)**
> Nine years since the mill stopped and I still count the lamps in the window every night. Goldie leaves one burning. One. I asked her why not two and she said two is for guests. So: one lamp, nine years, no guests.
>
> If you are reading this, you are the second lamp. Don't let that frighten you. It only means somebody kept the room warm until you got here.

**Entry 3 — "The Gear on Bram's Bench" (given Act II, after the first plate is read)**
> Bram's father worked the Works. Bram will tell you he doesn't remember them. He keeps a brass gear on his bench and moves it when he cleans and puts it back in the same spot, and I have never once seen him look at it directly.
>
> I don't think the town is afraid of machines. I think the town is afraid of finding out it misses them.

**Entry 6 — "The Day of the Water" (given Act III, when the shaft is drained)**
> Hollis was foreman. That is the entire fact, and he has spent forty years making it into a crime.
>
> Nobody died. Write that down twice. Nobody died. The shaft flooded, the crew came up wet and swearing, and the town decided that was a sign and closed everything and called it wisdom. Hollis let them, because letting them was easier than arguing, and then one day letting them had been forty years.
>
> If you get the water out, don't tell him it was nothing. Tell him it's fixed. He can hear that one.

**Entry 9 — "Why It Was Shut Down" (given Act IV, from the deep dark)**
> Otto will find the shutdown order eventually and she'll come to you white as a sheet, so let me spoil it: it was signed by my grandmother.
>
> She wasn't wrong to be careful. She was wrong about what careful meant. She thought she was protecting a town from a machine. What she actually did was take the heat out of the greenhouse, the flour out of the bakery, and the bees out of the orchard, and hand her granddaughter a valley that could only eat what the weather allowed.
>
> Careful is not the same as kind. I have been forty years working out the difference.

**Entry 13 — "For Whoever Rings It" (given at the finale, delivered by Pip)**
> The bell was never decoration. It was the resonator — the thing that told the whole valley the Works were alive, so the farmers knew the greenhouse was warm and the bakers knew the mill was turning and the kids knew supper was on. Taking it down was the last thing my grandmother did, and I have never blamed her for it, and I have also never forgiven it, and I have decided a person is allowed both.
>
> I got as far as the mould. I'm sorry. I ran out of years before I ran out of plans.
>
> So: light one lantern, then two. Feed somebody. Get the water out. Wake the thing under the square — it was only ever ours, it was only ever a furnace for a town, and a furnace is not frightening when there are people around it.
>
> Then let a kid ring it. It's their town now. It always was; we were just keeping the room warm.
>
> — Nan

# The pitch (paste into the group chat)

## Short

I built a Minecraft pack — and a whole valley to go in it, hand-built, that ships with it. You inherit a dead great-aunt's cottage in a valley where everyone leaves every February. Forty dark lamp posts. Fifteen neighbours who don't exist yet. One locked door in your cellar that says "Not yet." Your job is to make it through one winter. Spring's easy. Winter is why there's a nuclear reactor.

## Full

**Little Kettle Valley**

Josie Kettle left you a house. Three walls, a chimney, and a copper kettle over a cold hearth. Also a letter, which mostly says "I wrote it all down, start with the porch."

It's cozy on purpose and cursed on purpose. Every year for a decade, somebody packs up in February and nobody comes back in April. The valley has an inn with no innkeeper, a mill with a snapped axle, a marsh full of frog-folk who haven't had a neighbour in years, and forty lamp posts that haven't been lit since Josie was alive.

You get one year. Spring, wake the town. Summer, float lanterns on the lake. Autumn, fill the granary before Oda's ledger proves you'll starve in February. Winter, the whole valley goes cold and dark and boring, and the only way out is the thing Josie built under your cellar and shut down on purpose. If you make it to second spring, the town re-founds itself with your name on the deed.

Two ways to play it, same world. One of you cooks, farms, keeps ducks, fishes with Nella and decorates the inn. The other builds water wheels, then machines, then a storage network, then a reactor, then a quarry. Neither can finish alone: the boards for the first water wheel only exist if somebody dried them in Marnie's oven, and the tomato that grows in January only grows because the reactor is on.

It never asks you to be creative. The book always tells you the one next thing. Every quest hands you the tool for the next one. Nobody dies, there's no timer, and there's a bounty board that pays you in scrip so you can buy your way past any grind you hate.

Fifteen residents show up one at a time, each with their own thing going on. There's a kid who names all the animals. There's a frog who's cheerful with slightly wrong grammar. There's a prospector who explains everything twice. Bram says "Well." once in the whole game and it means everything.

Five minutes to install. It updates itself. **You don't make a world** — the valley comes with it, the same one for everybody, and clicking Play drops you on the road into it. Bring a friend or bring a spouse who hates open-world games; it was built for exactly that person.

Forty lamps. Fifteen people. One winter that nobody leaves.

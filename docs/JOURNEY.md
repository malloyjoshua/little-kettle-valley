# The journey: what a player actually does

Five acts, five seasons, 126 quests, about 16 hours of authored play (measured per quest, not guessed). Two lanes braid through it: the **cozy lane** (cook, plant, keep animals, fish, decorate, meet people) and the **tech lane** (Create, then Thermal, then Applied Energistics, then the reactor, then the quarry). Roughly every third quest in each lane needs something the other lane made. A solo player does both; a couple splits naturally.

The one line the pack repeats in every act: **Forty lamps. Fifteen people. One winter that nobody leaves.**

Three residents, a rebuilt house, a pet called Biscuit, a mystery door, the first machine and a hammer that breaks nine blocks at a time, before the hour is out. Every quest in that stretch is one to six minutes.

## Act I: Spring, The Thaw (20 quests, about 2 hours)
You arrive at a cold house in a valley that used to be a town. One neighbour sees the chimney smoke and decides you are staying. The cozy lane gets the kitchen running and the first crops in; the tech lane gets the mill turning with water wheels made from boards that only exist because somebody dried them in Marnie's oven. That is the first braid: she unblocks him before he has mined anything.

**Ends with:** the Thaw Fair. Oda reopens the store, five people live in the valley, six lamps are lit — and Marnie, on her way home, says the thing the act has been about: *"That door in her cellar. You've found it, then. Everybody who has ever lived in that house has found it. Nobody has ever seen it open."*

## Act II: Summer, The Long Days (18 quests, about 2.8 hours)
The town is awake and immediately too small. Summer is abundance you cannot store, water you cannot move, and light you do not have. Nella comes back to the lake and teaches you to dredge sand; Halden opens the hedge garden; the mill stops being a mill and becomes a workshop. Thermal machines arrive, and the first machine frame needs washed silica from lake sand only Nella's net can pull.

**Ends with:** the Midsummer Lantern Float. Paper lanterns on the water, ten lamps lit, the workshop powered — and Halden, on the pier, pointing the summer at the autumn: *"Josie stood on this pier the last summer she had and told me she'd bought a book about turbines. I laughed at her. I would very much like that back."*

## Act III: Autumn, The Harvest Debt (23 quests, about 3.4 hours)
The valley finally produces more than it eats, and Oda opens the ledger to show everyone the truth: at this rate the town runs out of food and fuel in February. Cozy work becomes preservation (pickles, jam, the granary). Tech work becomes storage: Applied Energistics goes in, and the moment the terminal links to Oda's board, every delivery quest becomes one click. The train line reaches town. Halden reads Josie's shorthand and the cellar door opens: she built a power plant, ran it for eleven days, and shut it down on purpose.

**Ends with:** the Harvest Supper. Twelve place settings, the whole town at one table, 22 lamps lit.

## Act IV: Winter, The Longest Night (23 quests, about 4.5 hours)
The crisis, played warm and never grim. Nobody dies. The valley gets cold, dark and boring: crops stop, the lake freezes, Marnie's stores run down. The cozy lane keeps everyone in blankets, tonics and hot food. The tech lane builds the reactor from Josie's notes, then the turbine, and the moment it lights, the greenhouse heaters come on and a tomato grows in January. This is the pack's thesis: the reactor is the reason food exists in winter.

**Ends with:** the Longest Night. Bram pulls the lever and the six nearest posts come up, hold, and go out again — a cold coolant line, named as one by Tobin and Bram before you have time to read it as a threat. Two seconds later the whole road lights at once. Fireworks over the Works, 39 lamps lit, the season turns, and Oda looks up at the ridge: *"There's a fire on the ridge road tonight… Nobody has walked IN to this valley in eleven years. Put the kettle on."*

## Act V: Second Spring, Founder's Day (19 quests, about 3.7 hours)
The valley survived a winter for the first time in a decade, and people start arriving on their own. The town formally re-founds itself, the books get balanced, the Works deed is bought with scrip earned by the whole town, and the quarry goes in. The fortieth lamp is on your own porch.

**Ends with:** Founder's Day. Fifteen people. Forty lamps. Three strangers walking up the High Street. Halden reads Josie's actual last page aloud — not the cellar wall the player read six hours ago — and the pack's last words are hers: *"when somebody new comes up the road next spring… go out and meet them. Bring bread. Pretend you were passing."* Then the world border opens.

## Oda's Counter (23 repeatable quests, always available after Act I)
The anti-grind valve. Bounties and deliveries pay Valley Scrip; scrip buys pre-made casings, servos, machine frames, reactor parts, and decor. Cozy labour deletes tech grind as a standing supply chain. If the tech player is ever stuck on a part, the answer is at the counter.

## Pacing rules the pack enforces
- **Every reward shortens the next task.** Tools arrive one quest before the dig they are for. Packets ship every block a decorate quest needs.
- **No quest over 25 minutes** of repetitive action. The three big dig-outs were cut to 40 blocks or come with an enchanted pick and TNT. Uranium was halved. The fishing quest asks for three species, not six. And Bram hands over a **Stone Hammer** in the first hour (Just Hammers): three by three, one swing, so nothing after Act I needs forty separate swings.
- **One pinned objective.** Chapters unlock one at a time. The next quest lights up the moment the current one is done, and its toast names it in plain words.
- **Never a timer, never a fail state.** The calendar is pressure; it is never a threat.
- **Everyone has more than one thing to say.** Each of the eight named residents carries a pool of five greetings, before and after their arc closes; the Ribbits and the three newcomers carry three each. Which one you get rotates by world day and by who is asking, so clicking a neighbour on day 40 is not the same sentence as day 3.
- **Payoffs render.** A person standing where they were not, a lit lamp, a warm room, a plushie. Never "the town is grateful".

## What the valley is built out of

Every building in Little Kettle Valley is a real structure template placed on a
levelled pad, not a procedural plank box. One style runs through the whole
town: **Towns and Towers `meadow_swiss`** for the houses, with **`exclusives/
classic`** filling the gaps that set does not have (the church, the well, the
farms). The inn is the **Dungeons and Taverns** standalone tavern piece, the
starting ruin is a self-contained `wild_ruin`, and the Works is fitted out with
`bunker` rooms inside its own sealed shell.

The layout is not written by hand. `tools/scripts/plan_town.py` reads the
template NBTs straight out of the installed mod jars, measures each footprint,
solves a collision-free plan around the Town Anchor, and writes:

* `media/town_plan.json` — the plan, readable
* `pack/kubejs/server_scripts/town_plan.js` — `global.valleyTownPlan`: the marks
  in `VALLEY.OFF`, the anchor- and works-relative command groups the finales and
  scenes run, and the verification probes
* `valley:setup/place_ruin`, `valley:act1/cottage`, `valley:act1/square_path`

so a pad rectangle, an apron, a chalked kitchen spot or an NPC's standing block
can never drift out of step with the building it belongs to. Re-run the script
after any change to `BUILDINGS`, `STREETS` or `RESERVED` in it; never hand-edit
the two generated files.

**The town.** A cobbled plaza 25 blocks across around the Town Square waystone,
with the classic well, four market carts, eight flower boxes and the four lit
corner lamps. Six streets leave it — the High Street south to the lake, Mill
Lane west, the Lake Road and Green Lane on the two southern diagonals, and the
two north lanes — each a three-block carriageway with a gravel verge, and the
verge is where the whitelisted lamp posts stand. Every door gets a cobbled
apron routed to the nearest paving and a lantern on a post beside it.

`scratch/verify_town.py <anchor> <home>` reads the finished world back out of
the region files and checks all of it: two blocks per template, every NPC's
standing block, the three waystones, the noticeboard and signpost text, the six
Act I lamps, all twenty-one player-placed post sites, and the Works — sealed,
dry, and floored with bunker stone. `scratch/run_town_test.sh` plays Acts I to V
on a headless server first.

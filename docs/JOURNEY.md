# The journey: what a player actually does

Five acts, five seasons, 125 quests, about 16 hours of authored play (measured per quest, not guessed). Two lanes braid through it: the **cozy lane** (cook, plant, keep animals, fish, decorate, meet people) and the **tech lane** (Create, then Thermal, then Applied Energistics, then the reactor, then the quarry). Roughly every third quest in each lane needs something the other lane made. A solo player does both; a couple splits naturally.

The one line the pack repeats in every act: **Forty lamps. Fifteen people. One winter that nobody leaves.**

Three residents, a rebuilt house, a pet, a mystery door and the first machine before the hour is out. Every quest in that stretch is one to six minutes.

## Act I: Spring, The Thaw (19 quests, about 2 hours)
You arrive at a cold house in a valley that used to be a town. One neighbour sees the chimney smoke and decides you are staying. The cozy lane gets the kitchen running and the first crops in; the tech lane gets the mill turning with water wheels made from boards that only exist because somebody dried them in Marnie's oven. That is the first braid: she unblocks him before he has mined anything.

**Ends with:** the Thaw Fair. Oda reopens the store, five people live in the valley, six lamps are lit, and there is a door in your cellar you cannot open.

## Act II: Summer, The Long Days (18 quests, about 2.8 hours)
The town is awake and immediately too small. Summer is abundance you cannot store, water you cannot move, and light you do not have. Nella comes back to the lake and teaches you to dredge sand; Halden opens the hedge garden; the mill stops being a mill and becomes a workshop. Thermal machines arrive, and the first machine frame needs washed silica from lake sand only Nella's net can pull.

**Ends with:** the Midsummer Lantern Float. Paper lanterns on the water, ten lamps lit, the workshop powered.

## Act III: Autumn, The Harvest Debt (23 quests, about 3.4 hours)
The valley finally produces more than it eats, and Oda opens the ledger to show everyone the truth: at this rate the town runs out of food and fuel in February. Cozy work becomes preservation (pickles, jam, the granary). Tech work becomes storage: Applied Energistics goes in, and the moment the terminal links to Oda's board, every delivery quest becomes one click. The train line reaches town. Halden reads Josie's shorthand and the cellar door opens: she built a power plant, ran it for eleven days, and shut it down on purpose.

**Ends with:** the Harvest Supper. Twelve place settings, the whole town at one table, 22 lamps lit.

## Act IV: Winter, The Longest Night (23 quests, about 4.5 hours)
The crisis, played warm and never grim. Nobody dies. The valley gets cold, dark and boring: crops stop, the lake freezes, Marnie's stores run down. The cozy lane keeps everyone in blankets, tonics and hot food. The tech lane builds the reactor from Josie's notes, then the turbine, and the moment it lights, the greenhouse heaters come on and a tomato grows in January. This is the pack's thesis: the reactor is the reason food exists in winter.

**Ends with:** the Longest Night. Fireworks over the Works, 39 lamps lit, and the season turns.

## Act V: Second Spring, Founder's Day (19 quests, about 3.7 hours)
The valley survived a winter for the first time in a decade, and people start arriving on their own. The town formally re-founds itself, the books get balanced, the Works deed is bought with scrip earned by the whole town, and the quarry goes in. The fortieth lamp is on your own porch.

**Ends with:** Founder's Day. Fifteen people. Forty lamps. The world border opens and the last line of the journal is read aloud.

## Oda's Counter (23 repeatable quests, always available after Act I)
The anti-grind valve. Bounties and deliveries pay Valley Scrip; scrip buys pre-made casings, servos, machine frames, reactor parts, and decor. Cozy labour deletes tech grind as a standing supply chain. If the tech player is ever stuck on a part, the answer is at the counter.

## Pacing rules the pack enforces
- **Every reward shortens the next task.** Tools arrive one quest before the dig they are for. Packets ship every block a decorate quest needs.
- **No quest over 25 minutes** of repetitive action. The three big dig-outs were cut to 40 blocks or come with an enchanted pick and TNT. Uranium was halved. The fishing quest asks for three species, not six.
- **One pinned objective.** Chapters unlock one at a time. The next quest lights up the moment the current one is done, and its toast names it in plain words.
- **Never a timer, never a fail state.** The calendar is pressure; it is never a threat.
- **Payoffs render.** A person standing where they were not, a lit lamp, a warm room, a plushie. Never "the town is grateful".
